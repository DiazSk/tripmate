Everything here is the `cli` transport (the default). The paid `api` transport was not run. Model
names are unchanged, and `MODEL` stays `claude-sonnet-4-5`.

## Accuracy evidence

- **Story.** I scored each script against the prompt's own rules: beat count and order, at most 55
  words, 2 to 4 sentences, a stop beat's first sentence at most 8 words, no digits or forbidden
  punctuation, no two "You" openings in a row. Across 3 days and 13 scripts per arm, the rule breaks
  per script were 0.15 at the default, **0.31 at 2,048**, 1.15 at 1,024 and 1.75 with thinking off.
  All arms parsed. The extra breaks at 2,048 were one 9-word first sentence and one stray
  punctuation mark.
- **Meri answer.** On the trip-chat prompt, thinking-off put a place *name* where an id belongs once
  in 3 runs. The default did it 0 times in 3, and a 1,024 budget did it 2 times in 4. So the gap was
  in the prompt, not the thinking. With the ids line added, thinking-off cited ids 6 times in 6, with
  the same months and sources as the default. On Home's prompt, which already had the line, months
  and sources matched across 3 runs per arm. `parseMeriAnswer` and `groundAnswer` are unchanged, and
  their tests pass. A name the evidence does not hold still replaces the answer with "nothing
  checked". In live curl runs against the dev server, all 3 trip-chat lookups answered with places
  taken from evidence ids.
- **Suite.** `npm test` passes 1,868 of 1,868. `tsc --noEmit` is clean after `next typegen`.
  `npm run lint` shows 0 errors (1 old warning in `travelerProfile.test.mjs`).

## Measured but not shipped

- **Generate + critique with no thinking: superseded** by the follow-up below, which kept critique
  at full thinking and tested generate alone. I ran 3 fixtures twice per arm through
  `/api/itinerary` (critique included) and scored the final plan with the bench scorers:

  | | composite mean (range) | wall p50 | generate p50 | critique p50 | critique revised |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | default | 0.894 (0.79-0.97) | 514s | 253s | 195s | 6/6 |
  | thinking 0 | 0.878 (0.77-0.93) | **173s** | **55s** | **46s** | 6/6 |

  Three times faster, and the composite difference is smaller than the spread inside either arm. But
  this is only n=6, the Barcelona plans came back with fewer stops (15-16 against 19-21), and
  `weatherFeasibility` fell on 3 of 6 runs. That is not enough to call quality held, so the default
  is unchanged. To try it: `LLM_CLI_THINKING=generate=0,critique=0`. A budget between 0 and the
  default (for example 4,096) was not measured. These runs had 6 generations in flight at once,
  which is why the baseline generate here is slower than production's 192s.
- **Chat with no thinking: rejected.** Edits ran in 4 to 23s against 24 to 134s, but 4 of 9 refine
  cells came back as prose with a `lookup` instead of a patch ("I need to look up…"), so they
  scored as failures. The default passed 9 of 9.
- **Idea-read with no thinking: rejected.** It is 5.3s against 11.4s, and the field-by-field scores
  were close (65-67 of 70 against 69 of 70). But it wrote the landmark as the question's place
  ("Treasury, Petra, Jordan") 4 times in 4, the geocoder cannot read that, and Home then answers
  nothing. A 1,024 budget bought no speed (8.4s against 8.7s).
- **Place-detail and context with no thinking: not adopted.** They are 10 to 13s against 13 to 27s.
  Both recall facts from model memory with nothing to check them against, and the thinking-off
  place card asserted new specifics ("cash only", "opened in 1900") that I could not verify.
- **Replacing the CLI system prompt** (`--system-prompt`) cut input from 9.3k to 3.1k tokens, but
  latency did not change (2.12s against 2.18s p50, 5 pairs). It would also change how the model
  behaves on every call type, and checking that would mean re-running everything above, so it was
  dropped.

## The 45s after generate, 2026-11-03

**The traveller waited for it, and the stop placing it was meant to cover never ran.** Before the
change, the streaming path sent the plan only after three things:

- the place-facts lookup (`fetchPoiOsmTags`),
- the dietary venue lookups,
- the stop placing sweep (`placeStops`).

All three shared one 45s `PLACING_BUDGET_MS` deadline. Only leg routing ran after the hand-over.

The stage frames of the 22 streamed runs above that produced a plan show the same thing every
time. It took exactly 45.0s from `generate:done` to the plan, and all of it was the facts lookup.
On the 15 runs whose destination geocoded, placing then started past the deadline and finished
in 0.0s, so stops kept the model's coordinates (the other 7 had no geocode, so no placing at all). Overpass is unreachable from this network today: `overpass-api.de` refuses the
connection, and both mirrors hang on a one-node query, while Open-Meteo answers in 0.7s.

**What the first render needs:** the stop coordinates (pins and the day map) and the forecast. The
listing facts and the dietary venues only become notes, pinned admission costs and the critique's
conflict list.

**The change** (`generationRunner.ts`, `api/itinerary/route.ts`):

- **Placing comes first and has the budget to itself.** The facts and dietary lookups run after it,
  with their own budget, so they also match listings against corrected coordinates instead of the
  model's (the match radius is 80m).
- **On the streaming path they run after the hand-over.** When they change anything, the enriched
  days go out through `live.onRevised`. That is the same frame and the same
  `applyRevisionIfUnedited` write the critique uses, so a traveller's edit still wins, and a client
  that has gone finds them on the draft. The phone reads the final plan from `done`, which carries
  them.
- **The route treats the enriched plan as the planned one once it is on the row.** Otherwise the
  critique's own write would find the row "edited" and drop its revision.
- **The critique still gets the facts.** It runs after them and reads `placeConflicts` and the
  dietary findings as before.
- **The non-streaming JSON path** keeps waiting for everything, in the new order.

**What it buys, and what it does not:**

- **Overpass healthy:** time-to-plan loses the whole facts query, and the facts arrive a few seconds
  after the plan opens. I could not time this today, because Overpass was down.
- **Overpass down:** the wait is unchanged. Placing now spends the 45s the facts lookup used to,
  because the last day's coordinate lookup starts as generate ends. The difference is that placing
  now actually runs, so a slow Overpass corrects pins instead of skipping them. Measured after the
  change, on Seoul with Overpass still down: the plan came 45.0s after generate, against 45.0s on
  all 22 runs before. The facts lookup then spent its own 45s in the background, so the critique
  started 91s after generate instead of about 47s, and its revision landed about 45s later than
  it would have.
- **Fail fast in an outage, built on 2026-11-03.** `askOverpass` now records, per mirror, an attempt
  that got no HTTP response: refused, or silent through its whole timeout. An attempt the caller's
  deadline cut short doesn't count, and neither does a 429, a 504 or a `remark`. Once all three are
  down within 10 minutes and nothing has answered since, `overpassUnreachable()` is true. Placing
  then skips its wait and its sweep, and the plan goes out on whatever the stream placed. The
  listing lookup skips too. The state lives on `globalThis`, because Next loaded `overpass.ts` once
  per route bundle: an outage `/api/roads` had just measured was invisible to `/api/itinerary`.
  - **Measured on Seoul, Overpass still down:** the plan came **0.0s** after generate on 2 of 2
    runs, against 45.0s before, and the critique started 0.5-0.9s after generate, against about 91s.
  - **One unexplained run:** right after the module was hot-reloaded it still waited 45s, with no
    state logged. That didn't recur.
  - **The first run of an outage still waits.** The marks come from a mirror staying silent for its
    whole timeout, so a run only skips once an earlier call in the same process has seen that.

## Generate's timeout, 2026-11-03

**Cause: thinking spikes, not prompt size or trip length.** From the 72 sonnet-4-5 generate traces
on both copies (69 finished and 3 timed out: production since 2026-10-01, and today's A/B):

- **Thinking decides the time.** A call is thinking time plus writing time. Thinking runs at about
  55-60 tokens/s and, with thinking on, ranged from 1.6k to 16.2k tokens, so the first word arrives anywhere from 28s
  to 264s in.
- **Writing is narrow.** It ran 3.3k to 9.2k tokens at 70-130 tokens/s, so 30-120s. It grows with
  the trip, by about 1.1-1.3k tokens a day: 3 to 4 days write 3.2-4.6k, and 7 days write 6.5-9.2k.
- **Prompt size barely varies**: 10.5k to 15k characters.
- **The cap is 300s plus 2s a day**, which is 308s for a 4-day trip. A 4-day call already
  succeeded at 305s, after 13.8k thinking tokens.
- **Both timeouts came from the heaviest prompts.** Production's two (2026-10-24) were 14k
  characters; today's was Barcelona, the largest at 15k, whose other A run thought 13.1k tokens
  and took 287s. Its stream carried no `stop` frame before the kill, so it was still thinking at
  308s.

**What the traveller sees:**

- **On web and on the phone with the stream intact:** after about 5 minutes, "Writing your plan
  took longer than TripMate allows, so it stopped. Your answers are kept. Try again." The
  allowance is already spent.
- **On the phone after a lost stream** (`planRecovery.ts`): the run reads `failed`, so it shows the
  generic "The planner didn't finish this plan" and the timeout reason is lost.

**Fix, built on 2026-11-03:** the 300s base stays, and the per-day term is set from the writing
rate measured above: 15s a day in place of 2s. That is 360s for 4 days and 405s for 7, and the
480s ceiling is unchanged.

- It covers the measured worst thinking (about 264s) plus a long trip's writing, and is still
  bounded.
- Raising the base alone would not bound anything: through the CLI nothing caps thinking.
  `MAX_THINKING_TOKENS` is a soft target, and B's 4,096 still produced 10.5k.
- **The phone's limit moved with it.** `RECOVERY_GIVE_UP_MS` went from 8 to 10 minutes. The 480s
  ceiling plus about 15s of setup and up to 45s of placing would have outlasted 8.
- **The trip chat uses the same budget** (`itineraryTimeoutMs` in `/api/trip-edit`), so a stuck
  chat turn now runs up to 13s a day longer before it is killed. Nothing about a chat turn that
  succeeds changes.
- **`claudeTimeout.test.mjs` changed in two places:**
  - Its snapshot of the slowest successful generate went from 220.8s to 304.5s. That is the
    current prompt era; two August calls on 30k-character prompts ran longer.
  - "Critique is not given less room than generate" now compares against generate's base. Its
    premise, that critique is on the traveller's critical path, stopped being true when the
    streaming path moved critique after the hand-over.

## Method

I took the prompts from `llm_traces` (newest 3 per type) and replayed them through the CLI with the
same flags `runClaudeViaCli` uses, varying only `MAX_THINKING_TOKENS`. Times are the CLI envelope's
`duration_ms` unless marked wall. Arms ran concurrently, so absolute numbers carry load. Compare
within a table, not across tables. The generation A/B used a dev server on a copy of the database
(`DB_PATH`), with `scripts/critique-ab.mjs` and `/api/bench` refine cells.
