Design audit · 19 Aug 2026

Why theirs
looks paid for.

Reference: vita-travel.webflow.io (Phenomenon Studio)  ·  Yours: TripMate, localhost:3000

Vita is one typeface, one hue, zero corner radius, and a scale that grows with the viewport. TripMate is five typefaces, four palettes, three aesthetics and a 1152 px box. Their restraint isn't taste you can't afford — it's a token file you can copy in an afternoon.

02 / Differences

The eight that
actually matter

01

One typeface. All the drama from size.

Vita

Inter only. The h1 is 3.2× the h2. Every bit of expression comes from scale jumps, negative tracking and 1.0 leading — never from a second family.

TripMate

Archivo 900 poster, Playfair Display scene headings, Source Serif 4 app headings, Manrope scene body, system sans everywhere else. Two or three families are visible in a single viewport.

Fix 
Keep TWO, maximum: Source Serif 4 (display) + one grotesk (body/UI).
Delete Archivo, Playfair Display, Manrope.

Playfair Display and Source Serif 4 are both high-contrast serifs
doing the same job in different sections. That is the tell.

02

The page should zoom, not reflow.

Vita

Root font-size is viewport-derived and every value — type, padding, gaps, radii — is expressed in rem. On a 27-inch monitor the whole composition scales up as one drawing.

TripMate

16px fixed plus max-w-6xl. At 1920px you get a 1152px column with roughly 380px of dead margin either side, while the photos go full-bleed. That mismatch is the loudest "web app, not magazine" signal in the build.

Fix — two lines, biggest single win 
html { font-size: clamp(13px, 1.13vw, 20px); }

/* then drop max-w-6xl and let the gutter do the work */
.wrap { padding-inline: 2.5rem; }

03

Negative tracking, not positive.

Vita

−0.078em at 11.9rem, tightening to −0.106em at 1.75rem. The letters pack into a solid block and the word becomes a shape.

TripMate

The poster headline is Archivo 900 at +0.035em with font-stretch 125%. Widened heavy sans is the single strongest template giveaway, and four centred ragged lines compound it.

Fix 
letter-spacing: -.075em;  font-stretch: normal;  line-height: .95;

Cut "Every day planned. Every dollar spent." down to ONE word set
huge, with the sentence as 1rem support text beneath it.
Vita's hero is literally the single word "Travel".

04

Sections need an opening chord.

Vita

Every section opens the same way: a 0.875rem/600 label pinned in the far-left column with a mark glyph, the display heading offset into column two, then a full-width hairline. You always know a new movement has started.

TripMate

No eyebrows, no rules, no column offset. Headings start at the same left edge as the body copy, so "How it actually works." just floats in the band.

Fix — the exact pattern 
<div class="grid grid-cols-[11rem_1fr] gap-10 border-t
            border-white/10 pt-10">
  <p class="text-[.875rem] font-semibold text-white/40">Journey</p>
  <h2>How it actually works</h2>
</div>

05

Kill the radius. Separate with rules.

Vita

0px radius on every card and image. No card fill, no border, no shadow. Separation is a 0.667px white-10% rule plus space. Only buttons are pills.

TripMate

16/12/4px radius plus backdrop-blur plus border plus shadow on nearly every element. Everything floats, so nothing is grounded, and the photo cards read as stickers.

Fix 
Images and content cards: radius 0, no border, no shadow.

Keep the glass treatment ONLY for things genuinely floating over
the globe — the itinerary panel and the map controls.
Keep the pill on buttons; that contrast is the point.

06

Card anatomy: title left, meta right, rule under.

Vita

Introvert Retreats left, / 78+ Countries right, on a hairline; image below at a fixed 0.92 portrait ratio; price and detail stacked in a two-column grid. The right-aligned meta with a leading slash is most of what makes it read editorial.

TripMate

The four Blue Hour cards are title over caption over image, all left-aligned, no rule, no meta, no ratio discipline — and the photos are visibly soft.

Fix 
<div class="flex justify-between border-b border-white/10 pb-2">
  <span class="font-semibold">The Blue Hour</span>
  <span class="text-white/40">/ 20 min, daily</span>
</div>
<img class="mt-4 aspect-[11/12] w-full object-cover">

Then replace the sources — yours are upscaled or over-compressed.

07

One hue, one accent, one product.

Vita

Teal-black base, one surface a shade lighter, white at 10/20/40/50/60/100%, and #fb9826 appearing essentially once on the whole page. Colour is punctuation, not a system.

TripMate

Three visual languages in one product: editorial blue-hour landing, glass cockpit app, then rotated paper postcards on /trips (#f4f1ea, drop shadows, random rotation). Plus two accents — copper #c9905a on the landing CTA, amber #ffb340 in the app — so the accent changes mid-flow.

Fix 
Pick ONE accent and delete the scoped override. Amber #ffb340
survives on the globe; copper on the desert does not.

Then retire the postcard skin or commit to it everywhere —
right now /trips looks like a different product.

08

Their "premium" is 60% choreography.

Vita

ScrollSmoother for inertia, SplitText for per-line masked reveals, ScrollTrigger for the hero's scrubbed collapse into the section below. Text arrives; it does not appear.

TripMate

GSAP and framer-motion are already in package.json. The landing fades. Nothing is masked, split, pinned or scrubbed.

Fix — best ratio of perceived polish to effort 
1. SplitText lines inside an overflow-hidden wrapper,
   y: 100% -> 0, stagger .08, ease "expo.out", on every h2.
   About 15 lines of code.
2. ScrollTrigger scrub on the hero image so it collapses into
   the band below instead of hard-cutting.
3. Gate both behind gsap.matchMedia() + prefers-reduced-motion.

03 / Craft

Defects that cost
you "expensive"

Verified against the source, not the screenshots. Five of the eleven items in the first version of this list were not defects at all — they are struck through below with what they actually were.

  • Chunky native scrollbar inside the itinerary panel, light on dark, full height — a 16px gutter down the brightest edge of a frosted panel. /trip/[id] · fixed
  • All four nav links are underlined by default. That is anchor styling, not a nav — and since everything in the bar is a link, marking all of them marks none. Every page · fixed
  • Day tabs truncate ("Day 2", "Da…") inside chevron shapes, with prev/next arrows crowding them. /trip/[id]
  • Native mm/dd/yyyy placeholders in the trip form, next to fully custom labels. Trip form
  • Emoji in generated copy inside otherwise editorial serif-italic prose. Itinerary text
  • Blue Hour card photos are visibly soft; Vita's are sharp at full res. Landing
  • The itinerary header photo is a 3:5 portrait source object-cover'd into a 4:3 box, so it crops to a slice that is mostly sky. Needs an object-position, not a bigger box. /trip/[id]

Withdrawn — not defects

Unlabelled "—" button, top-leftDevInspectorOverlay, gated on NODE_ENV === "development". Never reaches a visitor.
Compass overlapping "3 trips remembered"Preview-pane chrome, not the app. .map-controls is display:none on /trips, and the page has no fixed bottom-left element.
Two stacked compassesSame. MapControls mounts exactly once, from AppShell.
Empty dark block above "DAY 1 OF 4"The Denver photo loads and renders. The screenshot was taken before it decoded.
Poster copy on a heavy text-shadowDocumented, deliberate: .hero-legible's three-layer shadow exists so nothing sits between the type and the globe. A scrim was built, measured and removed by decision.

04 / Order

Do it in this order

Day 1 — free wins

Fluid root font-size. Drop max-w-6xl. Negative tracking on all headings, font-stretch: normal. Un-underline the nav. Fix the four overlap and empty-block bugs.

Day 2 — the system

Cut to two typefaces. One accent. Radius 0 on content cards and images, glass only over the globe. Add the label + offset-heading + hairline section opener.

Day 3 — the feel

SplitText line reveals on every heading. One scrubbed hero collapse. Replace the four card images with sharp sources at a fixed 11:12 ratio, meta right-aligned on a rule.

The reference has less in it than yours — fewer fonts, fewer colours, fewer shapes, fewer effects. It reads as more expensive for exactly that reason. Your fastest path is not adding polish; it is deleting variety until one system is left, then spending what you saved on motion.
